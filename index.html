01 / The inquiry

When does a new place begin to feel like home?

For one interview participant, campus life can feel welcoming without replacing the people and relationships associated with home. This project explores why that distinction matters—and whether other students describe it differently.

Main research question

How do international students at Texas Tech navigate cultural, social, and economic adjustments while developing a sense of belonging and identity in Lubbock, Texas?
One interview begins this inquiry. Its perspective is not representative of every international student.
Identity & cultureHow do people preserve familiar routines while learning new ones?
Relationships & connectionDo students feel connected to friends, to Texas Tech, to Lubbock—or to none of these in the same way?
Housing & practical lifeHow do living arrangements, finances, and transportation shape opportunities and limits?

04 / Firsthand research · Interview 01

Welcomed, but what counts as home?

The participant grew up in Nigeria and spent about two years in Virginia before coming to Texas Tech. Her story suggests a tension between a friendly university and the deeper relationships that make somewhere feel like home.

THE CENTRAL DISCOVERY

Belonging does not have to mean replacing home.

The student calls Nigeria her first home and Virginia her second, where family lives. She describes Lubbock as her third home. At the same time, she talks positively about everyday interactions at Texas Tech. In this account, being included is real, but emotional attachment to other places remains strong.

Read the interview transcript · 10 questions and a follow-up

Working transcript based on an edited subtitle transcription. Bracketed text indicates words that are unclear and need verification against the recording. This is not a time-aligned subtitle track.

Question 01 · InterviewerWhere are you originally from, and how long have you been studying at Texas Tech University?

Participant

I'm originally from Nigeria, but then I stayed in Virginia for about two years before moving to Texas. I've been studying at Texas Tech for [unclear—possibly two months now].

Question 02 · InterviewerBefore coming to Texas Tech, what did you expect life in Lubbock to be like?

Participant

I thought it was going to be boring, deserted, and, yeah, extra [unclear].

Question 03 · InterviewerWhat surprised you most when you first arrived at Texas Tech?

Participant

The community. Like, a lot of kind people, and it has a very good diversity rate. So there's always a place for everybody. And yeah, there are actually people here.

Question 04 · InterviewerWhat has been the most difficult part of adjusting to life in Lubbock?

Participant

When I miss my family, and there's not so much to do. Like, I mean, there is, but compared to where I'm coming from, there isn't so much to do.

Question 05 · InterviewerHave you experienced any cultural differences that affected your everyday life?

Participant

Not exactly. It's just like the people here are more friendly. I'm used to people being so focused on their lives in Virginia. So when I came here, people actually greet you. It was kind of different, but not life-changing different.

Question 06 · InterviewerCan you describe a time when you felt particularly welcomed or excluded at Texas Tech?

Participant

Welcomed? Like, you always feel welcomed at all the events. From the International Student Office to the normal everyday clubs, there will always be somebody to come greet you, come talk to you in every single activity. So I've never felt excluded from any events.

Question 07 · InterviewerWhat people, activities, or organizations have helped you feel connected to the Texas Tech community?

Participant

There's [organization name unclear], the National Society of Black Engineers, the Society of Women Engineers, and the African Student Organization.

Interviewer · Follow-upWhat about people?

My professors are pretty friendly. Then my classmates, they also get us to work in teams, so that helps you know more people. And yeah, the people here are generally kind.

Question 08 · InterviewerAre there particular places on campus or around Lubbock where you feel most comfortable? What makes those places special?

Participant

I like the library 'cause you get to see a lot of people, and it's nice to study. Then, to be honest, my classes actually feel comfortable and nice for me. I don't really go off campus, so [unclear]. Oh yeah, and the gym. The gym also makes me feel very comfortable and welcome. And then the Texas Tech games that I've been to, they're also really fun.

Question 09 · InterviewerWould you consider Lubbock or Texas Tech your second home? Why or why not?

Participant

I think [unclear]. It's my third home, because my first home is Nigeria and my second home is Virginia, because that's where my family members are. I don't really [unclear—reference to family or close friends in Lubbock]. That's just why I feel like the people [unclear] make a home, and the people that are making home for me are not here, so.

Question 10 · InterviewerLooking back on your experience, what advice would you give to a new international student struggling to feel at home?

Participant

I don't think [unclear] have problems, 'cause people will always talk to you. [Unclear—appears to mention making friends at international student events.] So yeah, I think it's important to go to events, not so many that you're distracted from your academics, but you should definitely go. There will always be people there willing to welcome you and stuff. Yeah.

Interviewer · ClosingOkay, thank you very much for your time. I really appreciate it.

05 / Inside the interview

What the participant actually discussed.

Explore the interview in a question-and-answer format. These are edited summaries from supplied captions, not a certified word-for-word transcript. Open each question to see the evidence; uncertain details are explicitly marked.

01 Where did the student come from?

The student says she is originally from Nigeria, lived in Virginia for approximately two years, and then moved to Texas. The length of time she has studied at TTU is unclear in the subtitles.

02 What did she expect Lubbock to be like?

She expected it to be boring and deserted. Her initial picture of the city was less lively than what she eventually experienced at the university.

03 What surprised her after arrival?

She points to community, kindness, and diversity at Texas Tech. The surprise was the presence of welcoming people rather than an empty, disconnected campus.

04 What has been difficult about adjusting?

Missing family is a major challenge. She also feels there is less to do in Lubbock than in the place she lived before, though she recognizes that activities do exist.

05 What cultural differences stood out?

People in Lubbock greet her more often than she was accustomed to in Virginia. She describes this as a pleasant difference, not a major life-changing cultural shock.

06 Where did she feel welcomed?

She describes International Student Office activities and everyday student clubs as welcoming, with someone willing to greet or talk with her. She does not recall feeling excluded from the events she attended.

07 Which organizations and people matter?

She mentions the National Society of Black Engineers, Society of Women Engineers, and African Student Organization. The name of one additional group is unclear. Professors, classmates, and collaborative assignments also help her meet people.

08 Which places feel comfortable?

She mentions the library, classes, gym, and TTU games. She enjoys seeing others at the library and studying there. She says she does not spend much time off campus.

09 Is Lubbock a home?

She calls it her third home. Nigeria is first, while Virginia is second because she has family there. The final explanation of why family and close relationships determine home includes uncertain captioning; avoid quoting that sentence until checked.

10 What advice would she give?

Attend social events and give yourself opportunities to meet people, but maintain balance so these activities do not interfere with academic responsibilities. Some wording in this answer is unclear.

What remains unasked

The interview provides useful information about community and identity, but does not establish her housing arrangement, transportation experience, or detailed financial adjustments. Those cannot be added as findings and should be investigated in follow-up interviews.

08 / Statement of goals & choices · developing draft

Why I am creating it this way

Purpose

To examine the difference between being accepted by a university and developing the deeper personal connections that make a place home. The first interview suggests those feelings may overlap without being identical.

Audience

International students, prospective TTU students, local students, and faculty who may recognize parts of the participant’s story while realizing that experiences differ from person to person.

Genre & organization

A scrolling interactive website organizes a real interview around arriving, adjusting, and belonging. Readers can explore places, switch between a three-stage narrative, and open each question’s evidence.

Multimodal choices

Visual illustration introduces the theme of travel; spatial design contrasts welcome and home; short quotations foreground the student’s voice; expandable interview notes and clickable sections support self-directed reading. An accessible audio player lets readers hear the consenting participant’s own voice, while an edited transcript supports readers who prefer text.

Rhetorical choices

I avoid describing all international students as alike. The participant reports kindness on campus but still centers family when explaining home. Leaving this apparent tension unresolved invites inquiry instead of forcing a simple success story.

Next revision

Cross-check unclear captions, confirm consent to quote, add campus observations and carefully chosen photographs, interview more students, document secondary research, and expand these notes into the separate 750-word formal Statement of Goals and Choices.
